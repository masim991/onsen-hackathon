Water types

Hot springs are classified by their temperature and dissolved substances. Properties vary; a water type is not a promise of treatment or a medical outcome.

These are indications in Japanese guidance on therapeutic bathing, not a guarantee of treatment or an individual result. Consult a clinician about health conditions and follow the facility’s guidance.

  • Simple spring

    A spring that meets temperature criteria but has relatively low concentrations of specified dissolved substances.

    Read more

    The classification considers temperature and dissolved substances. Japan’s environment ministry lists insomnia and autonomic nervous system imbalance among examples of bathing indications.

  • Carbon dioxide spring

    Classified by its dissolved carbon dioxide; composition and feel vary by source.

    Classified by dissolved carbon dioxide. The ministry lists sensitivity to cold and peripheral circulation disorders among examples of bathing indications.

  • Sulfur spring

    Contains sulfur compounds and may have a distinctive smell.

    Classified by sulfur compounds; a distinctive smell may occur. The ministry lists chronic eczema among bathing indications, but advises people with sensitive skin or mucous membranes to avoid bathing.

  • Acidic spring

    Has a low pH. Follow the facility’s guidance, especially if you have sensitive skin.

    Classified by acidity. The ministry lists atopic dermatitis among bathing indications, but advises people with sensitive skin or mucous membranes to avoid bathing.

    Contains iron and can change colour on exposure to air.

  • Free-flowing water

    Water is supplied from the spring and discharged; heating, dilution, circulation and disinfection can vary. Check the facility’s posted analysis.

    Read more

    Free-flowing describes how water is supplied and discharged, not its mineral classification. Heating, dilution and disinfection vary by facility; check its posted information.

Reading a water name

Source temperature is not necessarily bath temperature. Review posted signs and consult a clinician about health concerns.
